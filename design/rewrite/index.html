Prototype · pass 3 · the whole app

‘Almost One Screen’

Every screen in the brief, designed in place. The scene is the app; everything else is one tap away and folds back into it.

Keeping a painting is the reward
When a conversation ends, the moment condenses into a framed painting under a soft gallery light: its title, the time, one plain line on what you did and one on what Pip learned from you. Dismiss it and it flies into the control, which is where the wall lives.
The menu stays small
Where the town is right now, the wall, your words, settings and today's free time. Anything deeper grows the sheet to full height and slides in, the way iOS navigation does, and slides back out.
The wall and replays
Each painting is re-painted live from its place and hour, so the wall shows real light: dusk, lamp-lit nights, grey mornings. Open one to hear Pip's lines again in the voice, with your own lines in between.
No clock while you talk
Free minutes appear only in the menu. When they run out, the character wraps up at the next pause and keeps your place; the painting is still kept, and Plus is offered once, quietly.
First open is a conversation
No onboarding: the app breathes in on the burrow at the real time and Pip asks your name. The microphone is asked for the moment Pip first waits for an answer, with one line of why. Sign in with Apple is offered only after the first painting is kept.
