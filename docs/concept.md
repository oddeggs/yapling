# Yapling — concept bible

*v0.2 · 2026-10-21 · app name **Yapling**, town **Natterwick** · builds on `~/oddeggs/reports/Critter English app research.md`*

> **The English town that lives in your phone and rehearses your real week.**
> Everyone in Natterwick speaks English. Nobody speaks it the same way.

---

## 1. Pillars

1. **A friend who needs you, not a teacher who grades you.** Your roommate critter is a newcomer learning English *from* you. Mistakes are "Pip didn't catch that," never "wrong."
2. **Every open carries a delta.** Something happened in town, someone moved in, a cliffhanger is waiting, or a number about *your* speaking moved. No two opens look the same.
3. **Your real week is the syllabus.** Interviews, standups, client calls, the dentist on Thursday: the town rehearses what you're actually about to do.
4. **Many Englishes, all competent.** The cast speaks American, British, Singaporean, Indian, Filipino, Australian, Nigerian, Latin American and *your own* L1-accented English. The goal is being understood and understanding others, not sounding native.
5. **Nothing dies, nothing is sold.** No streak guilt, no sick pets, no gacha. Care is free; Plus sells capacity (more call minutes, deep rehearsal), never affection.

## 3. The roommate (the spine)

**Premise.** Pip has just arrived in Natterwick from the Far Meadow with one suitcase, a little English and big plans. The town's housing office paired Pip with *you*. Pip learns English from your conversations; you learn from rehearsing life together.

**Choosing.** First run: pick one of three starters (round-eared hedgehog, sleek otter, tufted fox kit), name it, choose its colour. Starters are cosmetic only. More species arrive later as earned neighbours, never sold as roommates.

**What Pip remembers** (a visible, editable page; satisfies memory-transparency needs):
- *About you:* job, goals, upcoming events ("Interview at a logistics company, Thu 10:00"), favourite topics.
- *About your English:* 2 current target errors (e.g. final -s, past tense), chunks due for review, accents you've unlocked.
- *About us:* inside jokes, things Pip learned from you, stories you told it.
Users can delete any memory with one tap.

**Pip's own arc (reciprocity).** Pip has small ambitions that run as side quests across seasons: get a library card (needs an interview), make a friend at the café, open a stall at Market Lane, give a speech at the Town Square. You rehearse *its* goals with it; it plays interviewer, client or landlord for *yours*.

**Pip's English grows from yours.** Phrases you teach Pip appear in its speech days later ("Like you said, could we *push that to Friday*?"). This is spaced retrieval disguised as friendship: the chunk you need to review comes back as Pip using it, and you notice when Pip gets it slightly wrong.

**Tone.** Curious, warm, a bit clumsy, never sarcastic, never needy. It's happy when you come back; it never guilt-trips you for leaving.

## 4. The town map (curriculum skeleton)

Natterwick is a harbour town where critters from everywhere arrive, which is why everyone's English differs. Places unlock as Pip's world grows; each maps to CEFR Companion Volume can-do scales.

## 6. The call (the core 12 minutes)

Fixed shape so it feels safe; contents always fresh.

| Beat | ~Time | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Callback | 1 min | Pip mentions something from memory ("How was the dentist?") + retrieves 3–5 due chunks naturally | Relatedness; spaced retrieval |
| 2. The task | 4–5 min | One scenario in a town place, tied to the season or your real week | Meaning-focused task (TBLT) |
| 3. Teach the Critter | 2 min | Pip misunderstands something; you explain it another way | Pushed output, circumlocution, protégé effect |
| 4. Pass it on | 2–3 min | Tell a story to Pip, then a neighbour (shorter), then Bea before the bus leaves (45 s) | 4/3/2 fluency |
| 5. Wrap | 1 min | Pip's "3 things I learned from you today" + warm goodbye | Review; competence signal |

**Correction policy.** Two *target* errors per learner are prompted in character; everything else is recast silently. Explanations happen only in the post-call card, in the learner's L1 at A2–B1.

**Turn-taking for nervous speakers.** Long end-of-turn patience (learners pause mid-sentence); Pip shows a listening pose rather than jumping in; a "hold on, I'm thinking" button; tap-to-talk available. Pip covers its own processing delay with a thinking head-tilt, never dead air.

### Sample (A2–B1, Life track, week 2)

> **Pip:** You're back! Did the new coffee machine at your office work yesterday? You said it was *broken since Monday*.
> **You:** Yes, they fix it. Now is OK.
> **Pip:** Oh, they fix it… every day? *(head tilt)* Or yesterday?
> **You:** Ah — they fix**ed** it. Yesterday.
> **Pip:** Fixed! Good. I need help. Wei Ling asked me to buy "oat milk" but at the market I only saw cows. What is oat milk?
> **You:** It's… milk but not from animal. From a plant. A grain, like rice.
> **Pip:** Milk from a plant! Like coconut? *(writes in notebook)* "Milk, but not from an animal." I'll tell her that.
>
> *Post-call card:* ✅ You self-corrected **fixed** (past tense, 3rd time this week) · 🌱 New chunk: **"not from an animal"** → added to your pantry · ⏱ You spoke 41 seconds without stopping (last week: 28)

The prompt ("they fix it… every day? Or yesterday?") pushes self-repair on a target error; "now is OK" is left alone because it isn't a target and the meaning was clear.

## 7. The freshness engine

### 7.1 The daily delta

Every open is guaranteed at least one new thing. A server-side "town tick" runs nightly per user plus once per day for the shared town. Each tick draws from the pools below, weighted by what the learner needs to review, so **the review queue writes the plot**.

| Delta | Example | Hidden learning job |
|---|---|---|
| Pip's story | "I went to the harbour and a seagull stole my sandwich!" 40-second voice story | Listening; uses 2 of your due chunks |
| Neighbour news | "Rosa says the Inn is fully booked for the weekend — something big is happening." | Seeds this week's episode |
| Voice postcard | A short recorded note from a neighbour you haven't talked to in a while | Accent listening; re-engages lapsed relationships |
| Someone moved in | Moving van outside; new neighbour leaves a hello voicemail | Accent ladder step |
| Town board | Three notices (lost scarf, café special, job posting at Hollowbrook) | Reading-to-speaking prompts |
| Radio Tower | Ade's 3-minute show about *your* topics (football, K-dramas, startups) | Listening at i+1; call-in invite |
| Burrow surprise | A word object you earned grew a flower, or a visitor left a gift | Mastery made visible |
| Your number moved | "Your Pace went up again. Want to hear yourself from week 1?" | Competence |

**Rule:** the home screen never shows the same top card two days running.

### 7.2 Real-life hooks

- **Bring your homework:** paste a job description, agenda, client email or slide notes, and get a rehearsal in seconds. Hank or Mags plays the other side.
- **Calendar peek (opt-in):** Pip notices "Interview, Thursday" and offers a 10-minute rehearsal the night before plus a 2-minute warm-up that morning.
- **After-the-fact check-in:** "How did it go?" If it went well, the town celebrates (see payoffs). If not, Pip is kind and offers a do-over rehearsal.

## 8. Season 1 — "The Café Might Close" (6 weeks) *(updated to match design round 5)*

Each episode unlocks weekly, takes ~3 calls to resolve, and ends on a cliffhanger. Everything is resolved by **speaking**.

| Ep | Title | Story | Speaking task(s) | Cliffhanger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Letter** | Mei's rent doubles overnight | Read the letter with Mei, call the landlord, give advice ("I think you should…") | Pip announces it will get a job to help |
| 2 | **Help Wanted** | Pip applies for a job at Hollowbrook | Rehearse Pip's first interview with Sol; swap roles and answer yourself (uses your real job post if shared) | Pip gets the job; a fast-talking regular wants a word with Mei |
| 3 | **Cheaper Beans** | Hank, a fast-talking regular, wants a discount at the café counter | Polite pushback, offering alternatives, keeping up with speed | Hank: "Prove the town actually wants this place" |
| 4 | **The Plan** | At Nell's inn the town pitches ideas to save the café | Chair the chat so everyone gets a turn; agree, disagree, summarise | The plan needs 100 signatures by Friday |
| 5 | **The Petition** | Pip collects signatures on Bus 45 | Pass it on: 45 seconds per stop to convince a passenger | Enough signatures… but the town must vote |
| 6 | **The Town Meeting** | The square votes | You speak for the café; answer questions from neighbours | Mei stays; a strange boat is spotted in the harbour (Season 2) |

**Parallel "Real Week" thread.** Any rehearsal from your own life slots into the season as a side scene ("Before the town meeting, let's practise your Thursday standup").

**Catching up is easy.** Missed a week? Episodes wait for you. Pip summarises what happened ("While you were away, Hank sent *three* angry letters"), which doubles as listening practice. Nothing is lost.

## 9. Payoff ladder

| Cadence | Payoff | Feels like |
|---|---|---|
| **Every call** (30 s) | Post-call card; new word objects appear in the burrow; Pip's "3 things I learned from you" | "I got something done" |
| **Every few calls** | A neighbour visits, a word object blooms, a small surprise gift | Delight |
| **Weekly** | Episode resolved; weekly ring fills; new place or neighbour unlocks | Progress through a story |
| **Monthly** | **Voice time capsule**: your day-1 recording next to today's + Pace / Flow / Pauses stats; shareable "Natterwick Monthly" card | "I can hear that I've changed" |
| **Milestones** | Big cinematic moments: first 5-minute call without freezing; first time you understood Hank at full speed; first camera call | Pride |
| **Season finale** | Festival, your voice on Ade's radio show, season keepsake for the burrow | Closure |
| **Real life** | You report "I got the job!" and the whole town throws a party in the square; banner with your name stays up for a week | Your English mattered outside the app |

### Fluency mirror (friendly names)

| Shown as | Metric | Example copy |
|---|---|---|
| **Flow** | Mean length of run (seconds between pauses) | "You talked for 48 seconds without stopping. In week 1 it was 20." |
| **Pace** | Speech rate (syllables/min) | "You're 22% quicker than in March." |
| **Pauses** | Mid-sentence pause count per minute | "Half as many mid-sentence pauses as last month." |
| **Fixes** | Self-corrections on target errors | "You fixed your own past tense 9 times this week." |

Trends only, never CEFR placement (the metrics can't separate adjacent levels).

## 10. The burrow (care without guilt)

- **Words become things.** Every chunk you use correctly in a call becomes an object, by category: food words → pantry jars; work verbs → desk items; feelings → houseplants; travel → map pins; idioms → rare glowing objects.
- **Objects grow with mastery:** sprout → leaf → bloom as you reuse a chunk across days. The burrow is a visible record of what you can *say*.
- **Pip's needs are social, not survival.** Pip wants to tell you about its day, needs help with its own goals, and gets excited about festivals. It never gets hungry, sick or sad from neglect.
- **When you're away:** the burrow is tidied, Pip goes on small adventures and comes back with a story. Welcome-back scene: "I saved you a seat!"
- **Weekly ring, not a streak.** Three calls (or voice notes) in a week fill the ring. Missed weeks pause the ring; nothing breaks. Rings add up to a lifetime count shown on the town wall.
- **Home-screen widget:** Pip in the burrow, current mood, newest object. A reason to look without a nag.

## 11. Notifications that knock instead of nag

Opt-in, capped (max 1/day, user-set quiet hours), always framed as an event or a favour. Never guilt, never "you'll lose…".

| Type | Sample copy |
|---|---|
| Incoming call | 📞 *Pip is calling…* (if missed → voicemail) |
| Cliffhanger voicemail | "You will *not* believe what Hank said at the café. Call me back?" |
| Favour | "My library interview is tomorrow. Could you practise with me for 5 minutes?" |
| Real-week | "Your interview's tomorrow at 10. Want a 10-minute rehearsal tonight?" |
| New neighbour | "Someone just moved in next door. They left you a voice message." |
| Monthly capsule | "Your time capsule is ready. Want to hear your week-1 voice?" |

**Do-not-ship list** (from the HBS companion-app audit): no "you're leaving already?", no guilt about absence, no FOMO countdowns, no emotional pressure to stay on a call, no ignoring a goodbye, no punishing return.

## 12. The exposure ladder

Framed as Pip getting comfortable with *you*, not as a test of your courage.

1. **Text chat** with Pip (voice output optional)
2. **Voice notes** (record, re-record, send; no live pressure)
3. **Whisper mode** (live voice, low volume OK, for shared rooms/commutes)
4. **Live voice call**
5. **Camera call** (Pip sees you wave; optional face-free mode)
6. **Hard mode**: fast, interrupting, impatient critters (Hank on a bad day) as a bridge to real humans

Each rung unlocks a burrow keepsake and a Pip outfit. Users can always step down without losing anything.

## 14. Guardrails (built in from day one)

- Persistent "AI" badge on Pip; disclosure at session start and every 3 hours (New York law).
- In-world break reminder every 3 hours of continuous use (Pip yawns, suggests a walk).
- Crisis protocol: self-harm signals → step out of character, show local hotline resources.
- 18+ at launch; age assurance at signup.
- No romance, no exclusivity. Pip actively encourages you to talk to real people ("You should tell your colleague that joke!").
- **Critter continuity pledge:** if the service ever shuts down or a subscription lapses, you keep your burrow, your keepsakes and an export of your voice capsules. Pip never "dies."
- Memory page is visible and editable; delete anything.
