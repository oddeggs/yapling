Yapling: walk into a quiet painted town that runs on your clock, and talk your way through it in English (iOS, portrait)

An AI English speaking app for Vietnamese adults and teens who can read English but freeze when they have to speak it. Natterwick is a parallel town that runs alongside your real day: open the app at 7 am and Mei is raising the café shutters; open it at 1 am and Pip, your red-panda roommate, is asleep on the sofa and wakes with a yawn. You walk into a moment that was already happening, and someone there needs you to talk. You speak out loud, hands-free, and the conversation goes where you take it. An AI story director quietly learns how well you speak and what you care about, and writes the next moment just for you. Every conversation ends as a small painting with a wry title, and the paintings you keep become your journey. No lessons, no days, no streaks.

Style: intentional minimalism at the highest level, a painted world with iOS 27 native glass blended in. Inspired by the painter smallwhitemonster's composition and colour, never copying their characters (no white long-eared creatures, black cats or skeletons). Each place is one muted colour field (dusk mauve, night blue, deep teal, oxblood, grey snow) with a low horizon, haze and two to four props; the figures are tiny in a big empty space; one small signal red in every frame (a neon 45, the sofa, Pip's red scarf). Light follows the real clock through nine keyframes (night, before dawn, dawn, morning, noon, afternoon, golden hour, dusk, blue hour). Paint, not vector: soft gradients only inside the painting, a matte grain and faint canvas weave over everything, no outlines. UI in three colours only: near-black #09080b, bone #f0ebe2 and ember #ff5b45. Spoken words in Newsreader (a book serif, large, like a film caption), painting titles in Newsreader italic in curly quotes, everything else small SF Pro with tracked capitals for labels. One piece of native liquid glass is the only control. Grown-up, quiet, tender; never a kids' app, never a classroom.

Characters: Pip (red panda), Linh (pangolin), Mei (otter), Sol (tarsier), Hank (raccoon), Nell (koala), Bea (bulldog), Dex (bison), Mags (heron). Each is one soft painted silhouette from ears to feet, never assembled from circles, no outlines, lit by the scene's light with a soft shade and a rim light, two small dark seed-shaped eyes and NO mouth. One signature accessory each (Pip's red scarf, Mei's apron, Bea's driver cap). Species details that read at small size (Pip's tear marks and ringed tail).

Motion: every movement on springs, nothing linear, nothing bouncy for its own sake. Characters talk with their bodies instead of a mouth: a small bend or nod on each beat of the voice, ear flicks, a raised paw for a question, a little hop on "!", a glance away on "…". While you talk they lean in, tilt their head and nod along; while they think they look up. Asleep, Pip melts into a loaf and springs back up when woken. Ears, tail and scarf follow through on their own springs. The camera eases in towards whoever is speaking and drifts back out after. Scenes change with a soft blur and slight zoom, like a breath. Their words arrive like paint: each word unblurs and rises slightly in time with the voice, in warm ink that dries to bone. Your words appear grey as you speak and turn white with a soft flash when final. Music, ambience and effects are made with ElevenLabs and follow the light; soft, rare haptics.

Features:
- Hands-free talk: the microphone is open in a scene, patient turn-taking, silence is fine, tap the scene to end your turn.
- Help by voice: say "again", "slower", "what does … mean?" or "tiếng Việt"; no help buttons.
- One glass control that morphs: a quiet hint when idle, an ember that breathes with your voice while you speak, and on tap three choices: say it again, pause, menu.
- Painting titles: each conversation opens with a wry title ("‘Waiting For The 45’") and closes with another plus "Kept · 24 Oct", the can-dos you showed and one thing Pip learned from you.
- The wall: your kept paintings as a quiet grid; tap one to replay it with its transcript, audio and Vietnamese.
- A living town: the bus comes at :00, :20 and :40, the café opens at 7, windows light up at dusk, passers-by cross the field, and small things happen while you're away (a burnt loaf, a postcard on the table).
- Your words: phrases you've said, each with the line it came from, free listen-and-repeat practice.
- Memory: everything the characters remember about you, editable and deletable.
- Free live minutes generous enough to finish real conversations; Plus for the full experience; running out is a kind in-character goodbye, never a wall mid-sentence.

Screens: first open (straight into the burrow at the real time, Pip asks your name; no onboarding wall), the scene with Pip talking (hero; show it at dusk at the bus stop, late night in the burrow and morning in the burrow), you speaking (ember control, your words appearing), the control's three choices, paused, the opening title, the closing painting card, the menu sheet (the wall, your words, settings, Plus, and where the town is right now: "Mei is at the café"), the wall, a replayed painting, your words, settings with memory, the Plus sheet, and the running-out goodbye. Also a model sheet for Pip: front, three-quarter and side, and six expressions made with eyes, ears and posture only (happy, curious, worried, laughing, sleepy, surprised).
