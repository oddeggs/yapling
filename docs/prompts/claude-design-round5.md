Yapling · Round 5: the learning journey, the story and the core features (iOS)

Round 4 is great: keep the Sticker + Glass direction, the brand, the cast, the component sheet and every R4 screen. We launch on iOS first, so stay on iPhone. This round is about what the learner experiences over weeks: how they grow, how the story pulls them back, and how the core features work end to end. Retire the older "Sticker Glass" board (it still says "Natterwick Plus", shows "Flow 74" without units and a sleeping Pip), so R4 is the one source of truth.

The journey: five residency stages, each changing how the town talks to you. Newcomer (A2), Neighbour, Regular, Local, Voice of Natterwick (C1). The learner never sees CEFR jargon, only "things you can now do". Each week has three calls that fill the ring: an Episode call (this week's story scene), a Real-week call (their own life: a job post, a meeting, "how did it go?") and a Practice call (Pass it on, Teach the yapling, pronunciation or an accent warm-up).

The story: Season 1 "The Café Might Close" (Mei's rent doubles, Pip gets a job, the mysterious Hank wants cheaper beans, the town meeting decides). Season 2 "The Mystery Boat" (a boat docks, a suitcase with your name on the tag, the Inn overflows, the boat turns out to be a travelling festival). Every conflict is solved by speaking, nobody gets hurt, missed weeks wait for you.

Fixes:
- Paywall minutes: Free is a generous first week, then about 10 live minutes a week. Plus is about 150 minutes a month, fair use. Update English and Vietnamese.
- Calls keep their place: Pip's calls show the burrow softly blurred behind; neighbour calls happen at their location (Mei behind the café counter, Dex in the interview room). No empty gradients.
- Correction is a prompt, not a fix: Pip repeats the error back as a question ("He fix it… every day? Or yesterday?"), the user says it again, and only then does "fixed" sparkle. Show both beats.
- Desk things are real desk objects (mug, sticky notes, stapler, laptop sticker, headphones), not plants in blue pots.
- First-language chips: Tiếng Việt, Bahasa Indonesia, ไทย, Português, Español, 日本語, 한국어, Türkçe, More.

New screens:
1. Placement result: after the one-minute first chat, Pip hands over a residency card ("Newcomer, welcome to Natterwick") with three things the user already does well and the first two can-dos to aim for. "Too easy? Too hard?" adjusts it.
2. The Me sheet (open from Pip's face, top-left of the burrow): residency stage, the can-do checklist split into Life and Work with ticked items, fixes you now own, What Pip remembers, notifications and quiet hours, Plus, help and safety.
3. Can-do keepsake: when a can-do is ticked, Pip draws a little framed certificate, signed by the neighbour who saw it ("Gave a 1-minute standup update · witnessed by Sol"), and it hangs on the burrow wall.
4. Stage-up: a town-square moment where the user receives a new residency card, the cast cheers, the town speeds up a notch and new places glow on the map.
5. The week view: the ring split into its three calls (Episode, Real week, Practice), each with a small illustration and status, plus this week's 3 to 5 new phrases.
6. The phrase lifecycle: a seed packet (heard it), sprout (said it once), leaf (said it again on another day), bloom (used it on your own weeks later). Show the bloom sparkle, and Pip reusing a phrase slightly wrong ("Could we pull that to Friday?") so the user can fix it.
7. Fix graduation: "Pip stopped asking about your -ed. It's yours now." with the next target gently introduced.
8. Listening checkpoint before a move-in: a 40-second voicemail from Sol and one in-story question ("Where did Sol say to meet?"). On success the moving van rolls in; on a miss Pip replays it slower together. No failure screen.
9. Real-week loop end to end: Bring your homework (paste a job post) → rehearsal with Dex → "Good luck tomorrow" → next day Pip asks "How did it go?" with three answers: it went well (town celebrates), it was OK, it went badly (Pip is kind and offers a do-over rehearsal).
10. Season map: Season 1's six episodes as a path through town with the current one glowing, finished ones showing a small keepsake, and the Season 2 boat teased on the horizon. Plus a key frame for each of Season 1's episodes and for Season 2 Episode 1 (the boat docks, the suitcase with your name).
11. Same scene, two stages: Hank's negotiation as a Newcomer sees it (slow, one simple request, a phrase hint) and as a Local sees it (fast, interrupting, no hints).
12. First-language help: tap any English line (captions, Pip's lines, postcards, story text) to see a short gloss in the user's language just below it, in the hand-written font. In live calls, a small "What does that mean?" chip makes Pip explain in simple English first, with the translation one more tap away. The tapped phrase drops into the burrow as a seed packet. App chrome, the paywall, permissions and safety screens are fully in the user's language.
