Yapling · Round 4: fix the model, fill the missing moments

Round 3 locked the style. Keep everything about the look: the cast, Pip's 14 moods, the mouth set, the palette, the burrow, the bus, the time capsule night scene. This round fixes a few things that contradict how the product works and adds the moments that make Yapling feel fresh, kind and alive.

Fixes:
- Brand: the app is Yapling; the town is Natterwick. Design a Yapling wordmark and an app icon (Pip's face, readable at 60px), and rename "Natterwick Plus" to "Yapling Plus".
- Paywall: the town, story episodes, Pip, the burrow and the time capsule are free. Plus sells capacity, never care: more live call minutes (fair use, not "unlimited"), Bring your homework, deep feedback and pronunciation drills, camera calls. Show a clear free vs Plus comparison, local pricing (Vietnam: ₫149.000/month, ₫799.000/year with a ₫599.000 intro offer) and a student discount. Keep "Pip stays in the burrow either way."
- Burrow home: every open must show what changed. Pip is awake and reacting, with a "Since you were away" card at the top (Pip's story, a voice postcard from a neighbour, a new episode, or a stat that moved) and a small mailbox by the door. Use the empty wall: a framed episode poster, a map pin board and a desk that fill up as words are learned. Show three word-object families (pantry jars for food words, desk items for work phrases, houseplants for feelings) in sprout, leaf and bloom states.
- Numbers need units: Flow is seconds without stopping ("48s, up from 20s"), Pace is words per minute, Pauses are per minute. Never an unlabelled score.
- Live call: add a small persistent "AI" badge on the critter's name tag, clear your-turn vs Pip's-turn states (listening pose with a soft mic glow vs talking with lipsync), and the in-character correction moment: Pip tilts its head, "They fix it… every day? Or yesterday?", and a gentle sparkle when the user self-corrects.
- Cast: give the residents more varied silhouettes (tall, round, tiny, long) so they read on the town map by shape, not colour. Add the Home Neighbour: a calm red-panda-sized neighbour who speaks English with the user's own accent, shown as the first friendly face after Pip.

New screens and moments:
1. Onboarding v2: pick your starter, then three quick choices (Life, Work or both; your first language; one goal for this month), a one-minute "first chat" that sets your level, a mic permission moment framed as "So Pip can hear you", "Can Pip call you sometimes?" notification opt-in with quiet hours, and an 18+ check.
2. Teach the yapling: Pip holds a notebook with a word it doesn't understand ("oat milk?"); you explain it; Pip writes your explanation in its own handwriting and thanks you.
3. Voicemail: a missed call becomes a voicemail card with a cliffhanger line, a waveform and "Call back" or "Later". No guilt copy anywhere.
4. Voice notes and text chat: the gentler first rungs for nervous users. Record, listen back, re-record, send, then Pip replies in voice.
5. Episode card and cliffhanger: the end-of-episode moment ("Next week: Hank wants it cheaper…"), and a "While you were away" recap that Pip tells you.
6. A neighbour moves in: moving van, boxes, a hello voicemail in a new accent, and the town map gaining a new face.
7. "I got the job!": report a real-life win and the whole town throws a party in the square, with a banner carrying your name.
8. Lock screen and widgets: an incoming-call notification, a voicemail notification, a home-screen widget of Pip in the burrow showing its mood and newest word object, and a Live Activity for an ongoing call.
9. Time of day: the burrow and town at morning, afternoon, evening and night, plus one rainy day, so no two opens look the same.
10. Gentle limits: the free live-call ration running out ("Pip's voice needs a rest until Monday. Voice notes are still open.") and the three-hour break reminder (Pip yawns and suggests a walk).

Also lay out a small component sheet: buttons, chips, cards, the nav bar, the weekly ring, the call controls, the type scale and colour tokens, so the screens can be built consistently. Show Vietnamese UI copy on two screens to check text length.
