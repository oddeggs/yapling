# Yapling — learning journey & storyline

*v0.2 · 2026-10-22 · adds the Visitor stage and Settling in (Release 1.1, `beginner-path.md`) · companion to `concept.md`. Evidence references point to `~/oddeggs/reports/Critter English app research.md`.*

The journey and the story are one system. **The curriculum decides what you need to say; the story decides why you're saying it.** The learner never sees a lesson list. They see a town that needs them.

---

## 1. The learning journey

### 1.1 Five residency stages, and Visitor before them (CEFR inside, never shown as jargon)

You don't "reach B1". You become more at home in Natterwick. Each stage changes how the town talks to you. True beginners start one step earlier, as a **Visitor**: the card shows no stage number, and "Chặng N/5" counts from Newcomer.

| Stage | CEFR | You can… (headline can-do) | Cast speed | Places open | Typical length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Visitor** ("Khách mới đến") | A0–A1 | say hello and your name, answer yes or no, ask for "again" and "slowly", catch a number and a price, order one thing, say where you're from | 0.7×, ~85 wpm, 8 words a turn at most | Burrow, Bus Stop, Café (Settling in) | Settling in (3–6 days), then week 1 |
| **1. Newcomer** | A2 | introduce yourself, order, ask for help, say what you did yesterday | 0.8× | Burrow, Café, Bus Stop | 3–5 weeks |
| **2. Neighbour** | A2+ / B1 | keep small talk going, describe problems, follow simple instructions, give a 1-minute update | 0.9× | + Market Lane, Office, Inn | 6–8 weeks |
| **3. Regular** | B1 / B1+ | explain your opinion with reasons, handle a standup, survive a structured interview, tell a story with a point | 1.0× | + Interview Room, Dock, Clinic | 8–10 weeks |
| **4. Local** | B2 | disagree politely, negotiate, present for 3 minutes, handle fast speakers and interruptions | 1.0–1.1×, overlap allowed | + Library, Radio Tower | 10–14 weeks |
| **5. Voice of Natterwick** | B2+ / C1 | persuade, run a meeting, host a radio segment, explain complex ideas simply | natural, accents mixed | everywhere, Hard mode default | ongoing |

**Placement:** onboarding first asks "Tiếng Anh của bạn bây giờ thế nào?". "Mình mới bắt đầu" makes the learner a Visitor with no placement call. "Mình nói được một chút" leads to the unpacking call (onboarding O5): a closed warm-up box, then three open boxes, and **Visitor if at most one of the three gets a fine answer**, otherwise Newcomer. It's a starting guess; the first two weeks adjust it silently. Users can say "too easy" or "too hard" any time; "too hard" at Newcomer offers "Tập với Linh trước" (become a Visitor and open Settling in) next to "Nói chậm hơn".

**Visitor to Newcomer:** at the end of week 1 (day 5's keepsake) Linh hands over the Newcomer residency card if at least 70% of week-1 turns passed without the generous pass. The learner can say "Ở lại thêm" and stay. The Me sheet's "Thử nhanh hơn" moves up any time.

**Two tracks, one town.** Life and Work share stages. A Work-track Neighbour spends more calls at the Office and Interview Room; a Life-track Neighbour more at the Café and Market. "Both" alternates.

### 1.2 Can-do checklist (the progress the user actually sees)

Progress is shown as **things you can now do**, ticked by evidence from calls, never by quizzes. Examples:

| Stage | Life | Work |
|---|---|---|
| Visitor | ☐ Say hello and your name · ☐ Answer yes or no politely · ☐ Ask someone to say it again or slower · ☐ Order one thing | ☐ Understand a number, a time and a price · ☐ Say where you're from and what you do |
| Newcomer | ☐ Order and change your order · ☐ Say what you did at the weekend · ☐ Ask someone to repeat or slow down | ☐ Introduce yourself and your job · ☐ Say you don't understand, politely · ☐ Give your availability |
| Neighbour | ☐ Keep small talk going for 2 minutes · ☐ Describe a problem to a shop or hotel · ☐ Understand directions | ☐ Give a 1-minute standup update · ☐ Ask a clarifying question in a meeting · ☐ Explain a delay |
| Regular | ☐ Tell a story with a beginning, middle and end · ☐ Give an opinion with two reasons · ☐ Describe symptoms at a clinic | ☐ Answer "Tell me about yourself" in 90 seconds · ☐ Walk someone through a process · ☐ Push back on a deadline once, politely |
| Local | ☐ Disagree with a friend without offending · ☐ Follow a fast, overlapping conversation | ☐ Negotiate a price or timeline · ☐ Present for 3 minutes and take questions · ☐ Handle an interruption |
| Voice | ☐ Persuade a group · ☐ Explain something complex simply | ☐ Run a 10-minute meeting · ☐ Give feedback to a colleague |

Each ticked can-do becomes a **keepsake** in the burrow (a framed certificate drawn by Pip, signed by the neighbour who witnessed it).

### 1.3 The weekly rhythm

The weekly ring fills with **three calls**, and each has a different job:

| Call | What it is | Learning job |
|---|---|---|
| **Episode call** | This week's story scene (see §2) | Meaning-focused task at your stage; the week's 3–5 new chunks |
| **Real-week call** | Your life: Bring your homework, a calendar event, or "how did it go?" | Transfer to your real situations; Work-track relevance |
| **Practice call** | A place drill: Pass it on (4/3/2), Teach the yapling, pronunciation pack, or an accent warm-up | Fluency, paraphrase, intelligibility |

Between calls (free, low-pressure): Pip's voice stories (listening), neighbour postcards (accent listening), voice notes (output without live pressure). These keep the "something new every open" promise without costing live minutes.

### 1.4 Phrases: the chunk lifecycle

Each week seeds 3–5 lexical chunks ("Could we push that to Friday?"). Every chunk moves through visible states, mirrored by its word object in the burrow:

| State | Trigger | Burrow object |
|---|---|---|
| **Met** | Heard in an episode or story | Seed packet on the shelf |
| **Sprout** | You used it once, correctly | Sprout |
| **Leaf** | Used again on a different day (spaced ~1, 3, 7 days) | Leaf |
| **Bloom** | Used unprompted in a new context 14+ days later | Bloom (sparkle moment) |
| **Wilting? Never.** | Not used for a while | It just stays a leaf. Pip or a neighbour drops the phrase into a story to give you a chance to use it again |

**Pip reuses your phrases** days later, sometimes slightly wrong on purpose ("Could we *pull* that to Friday?"), inviting you to fix it. That's retrieval practice disguised as friendship.

### 1.5 The accent ladder

| Stage | New voices | How they arrive |
|---|---|---|
| Visitor | Pip, Linh (your own accent, and she speaks your language at first), Bea and Mei in one short scene each | Settling in: Bea at the bus stop (Chapter 4), Mei at her café before the letter (Chapter 5) |
| Newcomer | Pip, Linh (your own accent), Mei (Singapore) | Linh is already next door on day 1 |
| Neighbour | Sol (Philippines), Bea (Britain), Nell (Australia) | Move-in events, one every ~2 weeks |
| Regular | Arjun (India), Dex (USA) | Dex runs Bring your homework interviews; Arjun moves in |
| Local | Hank (fast USA, full speed), visiting voices from the Season 2 boat | Hank appears in S1 E3 at Newcomer speed and returns faster as you grow; boat arrivals in Season 2 |
| Voice | Everyone, mixed, natural speed, overlapping | Town meetings and radio call-ins |

A **listening checkpoint** sits before each move-in: a 40-second neighbour voicemail you need to understand to answer a question in-story ("Where did Sol say to meet?"). Pass, and the van rolls in. Struggle, and Pip replays it slower with you; no failure screen.

### 1.6 Errors: two targets at a time

- The system tracks your recurring errors from transcripts (for Vietnamese speakers typically: final -s/-ed, past tense, articles, final consonants).
- **Two are active targets.** Pip prompts them in character during calls ("He fix it… every day? Or yesterday?"). Everything else is recast silently.
- A target **graduates** after you self-correct it 5 times across 3 different days. Celebration: "Pip stopped asking about your -ed! It's yours now." The next target activates.
- Post-call card "Your fixes" explains the target in your first language, one line.

### 1.7 The exposure ladder alongside the stages

| Rung | Default from | Unlock moment |
|---|---|---|
| Text chat + voice notes | Day 1 | Always available |
| Live voice with Pip | Day 1 (after first chat) | Onboarding |
| Live voice with neighbours | Week 1 | Linh's first call |
| Camera call (Plus) | Stage 2 | Pip asks "Can I see your face when we talk?" |
| Hard mode (fast, interrupting) | Stage 4 | Hank challenges you after S1 E3 |
| Real humans (later feature) | Stage 4+ | Optional speaking clubs |

Users can step down any time; nothing is lost.

### 1.8 How progress is measured and shown

| Signal | Shown as | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Mean length of run | **Flow** "48s without stopping, up from 20s" | Post-call, time capsule |
| Speech rate | **Pace** "112 words/min" | Post-call, time capsule |
| Mid-clause pauses | **Pauses** "3 per min" | Time capsule |
| Target errors graduated | **Fixes** "You own -ed now" | Post-call, Me sheet |
| Can-dos ticked | Checklist + keepsakes | Me sheet, burrow |
| Chunks bloomed | Burrow objects | Burrow |
| Stage | Residency badge | Me sheet |

Trends only; never "you are B1" on screen (the metrics can't separate adjacent levels). A **stage-up** is a story event: the town council (a.k.a. Mags the heron in later seasons) hands you a new residency card.

### 1.10 First-language help (tap to reveal)

Available everywhere, designed to fade, never a crutch:

| Where | Default | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| App chrome, paywall, permissions, safety, billing, settings | Fully in the user's language | This is product UI, not learning content. Trust and consent must be understood |
| Captions, Pip's lines, postcards, story text | English, tap a line or word → short L1 gloss below it | Tapped phrase becomes a seed packet (Met) in the chunk queue; no penalty |
| Live calls | English only on screen | "What does that mean?" chip → Pip explains in simple English first (asking for clarification is itself a can-do); L1 one more tap away |
| Live calls at Visitor | The say-this card from the start, two choices, Vietnamese under each; a Vietnamese task card when a new kind of turn starts | "What does that mean?" answers in Vietnamese first. In Settling in, Linh says the task in Vietnamese out loud (Chapters 1, 2, 3 and 6), then text cards. "Nói lại" and "Chậm thôi" replay the last line, never as a try |
| Post-call "Your fixes" | One-line explanation in L1 at Newcomer/Neighbour | English-first with L1 on tap from Regular. At Visitor there are no fixes: the card lists what the learner said ("Bạn đã nói được: …") |
| Listening checkpoints, Pass it on | No L1 | These measure comprehension and fluency |

**Fade by stage.** Visitor: line-level glosses everywhere, Vietnamese on every card, meanings Vietnamese first, and spoken Vietnamese from Linh in Settling in. Newcomer: line-level glosses everywhere. Neighbour: line-level on tap. Regular: word-level, English definition first, L1 one tap deeper. Local and up: English definitions only unless the user turns L1 back on in settings. Reveal frequency is tracked as a signal for difficulty, never shown as a score.

**Never:** a global "translate everything" toggle, auto L1 subtitles during listening, or translating the user's own speech back to them.

## 2. The storyline

### 2.1 Story rules

1. **Every conflict is solved by speaking.** Persuade, explain, negotiate, apologise, ask. No tapping to win.
2. **Low stakes, high warmth.** Cafés might close, luggage gets lost, a boat arrives; nobody gets hurt, nobody dies, nobody is humiliated.
3. **Each episode ends on a cliffhanger**; missed weeks wait for you, and Pip recaps what happened (listening practice).
4. **You matter.** Your choices and words shape small outcomes (the café's new name, what the town festival celebrates, which stall Pip opens). Outcomes always land well; how they land varies.
5. **Your real life bleeds in.** Real wins ("I got the job!") become town events; a rough real day gets a kind check-in and a do-over rehearsal.
6. **Same episode, different difficulty.** A Newcomer and a Local play the same scene; the task scales by stage (see §2.4).
7. **Mysteries pay off.** Silhouettes and rumours are revealed within the season.

### 2.2 Pip's own arc (runs across seasons)

| Season | Pip's goal | You help by… | Payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Get a job to save Mei's café | Rehearsing Pip's interview with Dex | Pip works at Hollowbrook part-time |
| 2 | Make a friend from the boat | Teaching Pip how to start conversations | Pip's new friend becomes a recurring voice |
| 3 | Open a stall at Market Lane | Practising pitches and bargaining with Arjun | Pip's stall sells things named after your phrases |
| 4 | Give a speech on the radio | Coaching Pip's speech; then giving your own | You and Pip co-host one of Ade's shows |

### 2.3 Seasons 1–4

**Season 1 — The Café Might Close** *(Newcomer focus: introductions, small talk, interviews, polite requests)*
Mei's rent doubles. Pip gets a job at Hollowbrook, Hank (a fast-talking regular) wants cheaper beans, the town makes a plan, Pip gathers a petition on Bus 45, and the town meeting decides. Finale: Mei stays and a strange boat is spotted in the harbour. *(Episode table in `concept.md` §8.)*

**Season 2 — The Mystery Boat** *(Neighbour focus: describing, complaining politely, speculating, travel and hospitality)*

Design round 5 titles: E1 The Boat Docks · E2 Whose Suitcase? · E3 The Inn Overflows · E4 Lost in Translation · E5 Lanterns at Dusk · E6 The Travelling Festival. The suitcase holds your invitation to the festival. The beats below are a draft to align to those titles.

| Ep | Title | Story | Speaking task | Cliffhanger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Mystery Boat | A boat docks; nobody knows who's aboard | Describe what you see; make guesses ("It might be…") | A suitcase is left on the pier with your name on the tag |
| 2 | Lost Luggage | The suitcase belongs to a traveller whose bags went missing | Make and handle a complaint; describe objects | The traveller needs a room, but the Inn is full |
| 3 | Speculation Station | The town gossips about why the boat came | Speculate, agree, disagree gently | Bea saw the captain at the bus stop with a map of the town |
| 4 | Full House at the Inn | Nell is overwhelmed by boat guests | Front-desk English; solving problems under pressure | A guest says they're here to "buy the harbour" |
| 5 | Who's on the Boat? | You interview the passengers to find out | Asking good questions; follow-ups | The captain asks to speak to *you* |
| 6 | Harbour Lights | The boat is a travelling festival looking for a home port; the town votes | Give a short speech for the festival; toast | The festival stays; a job ad appears at Hollowbrook ("We're growing!") |

**Season 3 — Hollowbrook Is Hiring** *(Regular focus: workplace English)*
Hollowbrook expands to support the festival. Pip is promoted to team lead and panics; you coach it through its first standup, a difficult feedback conversation with Sol, a client call with Hank, and a project presentation. Your real work artifacts slot into the season (Bring your homework). Finale: the Hollowbrook launch party; Pip thanks you in a speech.

**Season 4 — The Radio Tower** *(Local focus: opinions, storytelling, debate, public speaking)*
Ade arrives to restart the town's radio station. Each episode is a show segment: a call-in debate (Is the bus too early?), a storytelling night, a live interview with the festival captain, a crisis broadcast when a storm cuts the power. Finale: you and Pip co-host the anniversary show; past neighbours call in with messages for you.

**Between seasons:** a one-week **festival interlude** (no new story pressure): the time capsule, a season keepsake, the town decorated with things you said. Seasonal events (rainy-season café specials, end-of-year lights) are global and weather-based, never tied to specific cultural holidays.

### 2.4 How one scene scales by stage

*S1 E3, Hank wants cheaper beans at the café counter:*

| Stage | Hank's behaviour | Your task |
|---|---|---|
| Visitor | Slow, one short sentence, says what he wants first ("Ten percent off. Okay?") | Read one of two cards aloud: "Sorry, no." / "I'm sorry, we can't do that." Mei and Pip carry the rest of the scene |
| Newcomer | Slow, clear, one request | Say no politely using one phrase ("I'm sorry, we can't do that") and offer one alternative |
| Neighbour | Normal pace, two requests | Explain why, offer an alternative, confirm the agreement |
| Regular | Normal pace, pushes back once | Negotiate a compromise with reasons |
| Local | Fast, interrupts | Hold your ground, handle interruptions, summarise the deal |
| Voice | Fast, emotional, changes his mind | De-escalate, reframe, close the deal and keep the relationship |

Every episode script gets a Visitor row like this one: the turn becomes one or two cards, the character models the answer first, and lines stay under 8 words. Week 1's Visitor turns are written (`season-1-script.md`, Settling in); Episodes 2–6 get theirs in Release 2, and until then a Visitor plays them at Newcomer with "slower" on.

### 2.5 What the AI generates vs. what's written

| Written by us | Generated per learner |
|---|---|
| Season arcs, episode beats, cliffhangers, character bibles, voice lines for key moments | Dialogue inside scenes, scaled to stage and target errors |
| The cast's personalities and accents | Pip's stories and neighbour postcards (seeded with your due chunks) |
| Safety rails and tone rules | Real-week scenes from your homework and calendar |
| Can-do checklist and stage gates | Recaps, voicemails, "how did it go?" follow-ups |

Key story beats are **pre-written and pre-recorded** where possible (cheaper, consistent, better acting). Free conversation inside a beat is generated.

### 2.6 After Season One: the story engine

From Release 3, the weeks between hand-written seasons come from the story engine ([`story-engine.md`](story-engine.md)). Each week a strong model writes one town week per stage from a fixed backbone prompt, the level guide, the town canon and a few screened, Natterwick-ised news items. Code checks it, a person reviews it, and the cast voices record it. A cheap personal pass then adds each learner's real-week and practice calls, their phrases and their burrow props. The scenes use the same `Scene` format and runner as Season One. The table in §2.5 still holds: seasons, tentpoles, the cast and the rules stay written by us. The engine also feeds the visual library (~100 scenes and ~1,000 props, each prop a word), the album and the share cards. Whether town festivals may follow the real calendar's big moments (§2.3 says not) is decision 3 in that doc.
