# Natterwick, grown: twenty-one neighbours and a hundred places

Written 25 Oct 2026 for the rewrite (`docs/rewrite/brief.md`). The founder asked for at least 20 characters and 100 places at launch, so that every learner's stories stay their own and can keep growing for months. This document is the design; the same content is data in `design/rewrite/world/cast.json` and `design/rewrite/world/places.json`, ready to be imported into `@yapling/scene` and the director's bible later.

Nothing here changes the existing nine characters or the twelve painted places. They are copied in as they are, with new places added for them (Bea gets her first four). Where this document proposes something about an existing character that the cast bible doesn't say (a want, a secret, an arc), it is a proposal, marked as one in §9.

**At a glance**

- **21 characters:** the existing 9 and 12 new neighbours. Three of the new ones were already sketched in the cast bible's "Later cast" (Arjun, Ade, Professor Moss); nine are new.
- **100 places** in **10 districts**, each with a resident who keeps hours, three variants, a music and ambience prompt and an art note.
- Every arrival goal and every hard situation has **at least two new characters** who rehearse it, as well as the existing ones (§4).
- The director gets rules for unlocking the world by stage, growing friendships over many stories, district seasons and not repeating itself (§8).

## 1. Rules this world keeps

Everything follows the brief and the bible. In short:

- **Painted dusk.** Each place is one muted colour field, a low horizon, haze and grain, two to four props, tiny figures in a big empty space, and **one small signal red**. Light comes from the town clock; weather and season add variants. Each place has its own composition: no two are the same painting with a different prop.
- **One soft body.** Every critter is one painted silhouette, never built from circles, no outlines, no mouths. The species below were chosen so that the 21 silhouettes read differently at thumbnail size (§3.3), and each has one accessory that hangs on springs and one habit for the animator.
- **Accents live in the voice.** Lines are standard English. No phonetic spelling, no broken grammar. A character may have **one light regional word**, used at most once a scene and always clear from context (Mei's "lah", Bea's "love", Orla's "grand", Thandi's "just now"); nobody else gets one. Three new neighbours (Rafa, Joon, Elif) learned English as adults: their English is fluent and correct, and their accent is their own. Every voice gets a native-speaker review before launch.
- **Competent and warm.** Everyone is good at their job and kind. Nobody is the joke, no accent is the joke, no species is the joke.
- **Low stakes, high warmth.** No real holidays, brands, politics, religion, war, crime or danger; no romance; nobody gets frighteningly ill. The clinic is for check-ups, a sore wrist and a pharmacy label; the bank is for opening an account, never money trouble for the learner. Natterwick has its own festivals (§7).
- **Every conflict is solved by speaking.**

### 2.1 The twelve new neighbours

#### Arjun · Bengal tiger · `arjun`

- **Role:** Runs the spice-and-everything stall on Market Lane, a tea cart at the station at dawn and a table at the night market.
- **Age, pronoun:** 34, he
- **English:** Indian English: articulate, quick, musical.
- **Personality:** Charming and quick; treats bargaining as a game both sides should win.
- **Wants:** To turn his stall into a real shop, with a door and a bell, before the First Frost Market.
- **Secret / soft spot:** Writes tiny poems on the paper spice twists and hopes nobody notices; he signs none of them.
- **Rehearses:** bargaining; prices and numbers; describing a thing when you don't know its name; asking for alternatives; recommending; shopping abroad; speaking for the stallholders at a town meeting.
- **Goals it serves:** Travel, Making friends, Moving abroad, Family. **Hard situations:** Finding words, Fast speech, Small talk, Meetings.
- **Accessory:** a striped cloth bag of paper spice twists across his chest. **Habit:** lifts a pinch of something to his nose and closes his eyes before he names a price.
- **Silhouette:** big low striped cat, heavy paws, long tail; reads as a broad soft wedge, not a red panda (no rings, much bigger).
- **Day:** Spice loft 5–6 am; tea cart at the station 6–8:30 am; the stall 9 am–7 pm; night market 7–11 pm.
- **Places:** `spicestall`, `spiceloft`, `nightmarket`, `chaicart`
- **Arc over many stories:** Bargains with the learner for fun, and loses gracefully → The learner finds a poem on a spice twist and works out who wrote it → Opens the shop door on the First Frost Market; the poems go up on the wall.
- **Ties:** Joon: Joon is the only customer who never haggles, which drives Arjun mad; Mags: needs a shop from the property office.
- **Sample:** "For you, special price. But first, tell me what you are cooking, and for whom."
- **Voice (ElevenLabs):** Male, early 30s; warm light baritone; quick, musical rhythm, a smile in every bargain; natural pace ~150 wpm. Indian English, native-speaker review.

## 8. How the director uses the world

The director already plans a five-scene story across two to four places with two or three neighbours (`craft.ts`). These rules decide *which* neighbours and places, so stories stay unique and grow with the learner.

### 8.1 The world opens as the learner grows

Each character has a `stage` (when they can first be central in a story) and each place has a `stage` (when a scene there can be central). Before that, a character can still pass through a scene, be mentioned or wave from the 45; they just don't carry a story.

| Stage | Who becomes central | What the world feels like |
|---|---|---|
| **Visitor** | Pip, Linh, Mei, Sol, Hank, Nell, Bea, Dex, Mags, and the two easiest new faces: **Joon** (order one thing) and **Azlan** (a stamp, a number, a name) | Home and the easy loop: the burrow, the café, the 45 and its stops, the library, the noodle stall, the post office, the Copper Pot, the breakfast room, the arcade, the duck pond |
| **Newcomer** | **Orla, Rafa, Elif, Marnie** | Everyday errands and first friendships: the bank, the station desk, airfield arrivals, the pool and the gym, the garden, the school gate, the inn desk, the office stand-up, the canteen |
| **Neighbour** | **Arjun, Ade, Amani, Norma** (Arjun moves in at Neighbour, as the cast bible says) | Town life and work: the market and night market, the radio, the clinic and pharmacy, the ferry, the hotel, the Repair Café, meeting room B, the town planning meeting |
| **Regular** | **Thandi, Professor Moss** | Bigger asks: the lecture hall and exam room, the theatre and open mic, the Shed and the lift, the flat viewing, the lighthouse, the night crossing |
| **Local** | (everyone) | The hardest rooms: the boardroom, the pitch room on Demo Day, the phone pods with the Far Shore, the town hall vote |
| **Voice** | (everyone) | **The roles flip.** The learner runs the room: chairs the town meeting, co-hosts The Night Desk, gives the lecture Moss usually gives, teaches Joon's class, sits on Thandi's hiring panel, calls the stroke in the Harbour Race, examines Pip. Never make them the student again. |

**Goal pull.** The learner's main goal can bring its neighbours in early. Any character whose `goals` include the learner's first goal is available from Newcomer, and their places count as two stages lower (never below Newcomer). So a learner preparing for an exam meets Professor Moss at Newcomer on the quad or in office hours, and a learner preparing for an interview meets Dex and Joon's hiring story early. At Visitor, the goal touches the story only through the existing cast.

**New faces.** At most one new neighbour is introduced per story, introduced by someone the learner already knows ("Go and see Azlan at the post office; tell him I sent you"). In every scene, either Pip or a neighbour the learner has met is present.

### 8.2 Friendships that grow over many stories

Story memory keeps a **bond** with each neighbour:

| Bond | What it means | What the director may do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 · seen | passed them, heard of them | mention, cameo, wave |
| 1 · met | one scene together | central in a story; they remember one thing the learner said |
| 2 · friend | helped them once | they share their **want**; they turn up to help in other neighbours' stories |
| 3 · confidant | helped them twice, in different stories | their **secret** can come out, as a story's turn or emotional core |
| 4 · ally | after the secret | their **arc** pays off; they help with the learner's own goal (Dex runs a real rehearsal, Orla plans the learner's real trip); at Voice they hand the learner a role |

Bond rises at most one step per story. A secret is never revealed before bond 3, and an arc's last beat never before bond 4. Each character's `arc` in `cast.json` is three or four story-sized beats; the director plays them in order across months, with other stories in between. A neighbour remembers the learner's own things (job, interests, home country, earlier stories) and uses one of them each time they meet.

### 8.3 District seasons

In the month a district is in season (§7), the director should set one scene of a story there when it fits the learner's spine, and use the festival as a backdrop or a deadline ("the Harbour Race is Saturday"). It never forces a festival onto a story that doesn't need it, and a district's season is never the reason for a story about that festival twice in a row.

### 8.4 Not repeating itself

Story memory keeps, for each story: its **premise family**, its **twist type**, its **central neighbour**, the **cast**, the **places and variants** used, and the **districts**. When planning, the director checks:

1. **Premise family** not used in the last 4 stories. The families: a lost or misdelivered thing; a place under (low-stakes) threat; a first time or a performance (a talk, a pitch, the open mic, an exam); a visitor arrives; a misunderstanding (a misread sign, swapped labels, a wrong order); a secret project or surprise; a competition (the race, the trophy, the high score); something broken to fix; a plan or booking gone sideways; helping someone ask for help; a message to carry or explain; an event to organise.
2. **Twist type** not used in the last 3 stories. The types: hidden kindness (Hank and the beans); the helper needed help; it was a gift all along; someone was practising in secret; the stranger is someone's old friend or teacher; the "problem" was a misunderstanding of a word; the thing was kept on purpose.
3. **Central neighbour** not the same as the last story's, and at most twice in the last 6 (Pip isn't counted; Pip is in every story).
4. **Places:** a story's set of places shares at most one place with any of the last 6 stories (the burrow doesn't count). A non-home place is a scene at most once in 3 stories. A variant isn't reused for 10 stories unless it is a deliberate callback.
5. **Spread:** a story uses 2 or 3 districts, and the last 3 stories between them touch at least 4.
6. **Pairings:** the same pair of neighbours doesn't share a story more than twice in the last 8, so ties (§3.1) feel like discoveries, not a fixed double act.
7. **Callbacks:** every story brings back one thing from an earlier one (a person, a promise, an object, a word the learner taught Pip).

With 21 neighbours, about 300 variants, 12 premise families and 7 twist types, these rules still leave thousands of different stories before anything repeats, and the bond ladder means the same neighbour is a different story the third time you meet them.

### 8.5 Feeding the bible

When this goes into code, `world.ts` grows two things: the cast lines for the 12 new neighbours (role, English, personality, one or two signature lines, as for the existing nine), and the place lines generated from `PLACES` as now. To keep the cached prefix a sensible size, the director could see the full place list only for the districts the story may use (§8.4), plus a one-line index of the rest.

## 9. Open questions and things I was unsure about

- **Proposed details for the existing nine.** Wants, secrets, ties, arcs and the new places for Pip, Linh, Mei, Sol, Hank, Nell, Bea, Dex and Mags are proposals; the cast bible doesn't say. Two come from the bible and `craft.ts` already (Mei's rent, Hank's beans, Mags's kept letter); the others (Bea has never ridden the 45, Nell has never taken a holiday, Dex's list, Linh's own exam, Sol's phone-box rehearsals) are new.
- **The cast bible's "Later cast".** Arjun, Ade and Professor Moss are used as sketched, but Moss moves from the library to University Hill (the library stays Linh's), and his "slow Scottish" became a full character. Tala (sea turtle, clinic, Filipino English) is not used: Filipino English is already Sol's, and a sea turtle is too close to Moss's tortoise; Amani takes the clinic.
- **Arjun's stage.** Kept at Neighbour, as the bible says. His tea cart is natural early practice for prices and numbers; moving him to Newcomer would be easy if the founder wants it.
- **Species and home.** Like the existing cast (koala and Australian, tarsier and Filipino), some new species nod to the voice's home (tiger, meerkat, moose, Malayan tapir, hornbill) and some don't (walrus, owl, zebra, bear, hedgehog, elephant). I avoided any pairing that could read as a comment on the people, but the founder and native reviewers should look at it as a whole.
- **Close silhouettes.** Elephant and tapir (trunks), hedgehog and pangolin (small, textured backs), tiger and red panda (orange), and three birds. §3.3 says how each pair separates; worth testing as painted thumbnails before the art is final.
- **Music with a regional flavour.** A few place prompts lean towards the resident's home (bossa nova at the beach, a one-drop groove at the ferry terminal, a thumb piano on the towpath). They're meant as warmth, not costume; if they feel like costume in review, swap them for neutral instruments.
- **Pip and the existing "any time" places.** The library, the platform, the cinema and Pip's places are open any time, as in the code. The narrower-window rule (§3.2) handles overlaps; if the town clock ever needs a single answer to "where is Linh at 8 pm", it should come from `present` windows, with the library as her fallback.
- **No weekdays.** The town clock has dates but no weekdays, so weekly things are daily windows or variants. If weekdays come later, the cooking class, the Swap Shop, Demo Day and fair days are the first to move.
- **Northern months.** The calendar follows the real month, as the weather does, so a learner in Australia gets the First Frost Market in their summer. That is consistent with the town having its own weather, but worth a founder decision.
- **Names.** Ids are single lowercase words. `norma` and `moss` are shown as "Captain Norma" and "Professor Moss" in the UI.

## 10. The data

- `design/rewrite/world/places.json`: `{ version, note, districts[], places[] }`. Each place has the fields of `Place` in `packages/scene/src/places.ts` (`id, name, resident, hours, story, variants[3]{label, prop, title}, music, ambience, interior`), plus `district`, `art`, `present` (hour windows when the resident is painted there, `[start, end)` on a 24-hour clock, wrapping past midnight), `stage`, `existing`, and `keys` (the current audio file keys) for the twelve existing places. `music` and `ambience` are prompts.
- `design/rewrite/world/cast.json`: `{ version, note, cast[] }`. Each character has `id, name, species, role, accent, personality, want, secret, teaches[], accessory, habit, hours, places[], sample, voice`, plus `goals[]` and `hard[]` (arrival ids from `craft.ts`), `stage`, `age`, `pronoun`, `silhouette`, `marker` (the one light regional word, if any), `arc[]`, `ties[]` and `existing`.
- `places[]` on each character is derived from the places' `resident`, so the two files always agree.
